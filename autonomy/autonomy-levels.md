# Levels of Drone Autonomy

"Autonomous" is a marketing word until you define what the machine does without
you, in what conditions, and what it does when it runs out of answers. This
chapter is a levels framework that pins all three down — and, unlike most such
charts, it names the **real hardware** that is the minimum to honestly claim each
level with 2026 technology.

The structure borrows from **SAE J3016** (the 0–5 ladder the self-driving
industry standardized on) and the **Exyn aerial-autonomy levels** that adapted it
for drones. We keep their two best ideas — the cumulative ladder and the split
between "pilot is flying" and "operator is not flying" — and add the two rows
those charts leave out: the **ODD** (where the level is actually valid) and the
**fallback** (what happens when it fails). The last one matters most. A level
claim with no defined failure behavior is the thing that puts an aircraft into a
wall.

## How to read the chart

- **Cumulative.** Each level contains every capability below it. A Level 4A
  drone can still do everything a Level 2 drone does; it just does more.
- **The dashed line at Level 3 is the real boundary.** To its left the pilot is
  flying the aircraft and the system assists. To its right the operator is *not*
  flying — they task the system (a waypoint, an area, an objective) and it
  executes. Crossing that line is a step change in liability, not just capability.
- **Two rows we added.** *ODD* (Operational Design Domain) is the envelope the
  level is valid in — GPS, light, degraded-visual conditions (dust/smoke/fog),
  link, indoor/outdoor/subterranean. *Fallback* is the minimal-risk behavior when
  an input degrades. SAE defines the fallback explicitly; most drone-autonomy
  charts quietly drop it. We don't.

## What we changed from the source frameworks

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Replaced "limited / all conditions" with an explicit **ODD** row | "Limited" is unfalsifiable. An ODD (GPS-denied? dark? dusty? indoor?) is something you can test against and certify to. |
| Added a **Fallback / minimal-risk** row | The level you can *claim* is the level whose failure mode you've defined. Lost link, dead SLAM, low battery — each needs a defined safe state. |
| Added a **hardware-minimum** row | Autonomy claims drift toward fiction without a parts list. This row is the floor: below it, the level is not physically reachable. |
| Mapped each level to the **Prismo stack** (see table below) | So the ladder doubles as our own capability roadmap, not just a taxonomy. |

## Hardware minimums, level by level

The headline row of the chart. Each entry is the *minimum* current-tech bill of
materials that crosses the bar — and the failure mode that stops it short of the
next level. Hardware is illustrative of a class, not an endorsement.

- **L0 — gyro only.** Any Betaflight rate-mode flight controller (a SpeedyBee
  F405-class board). It damps rotation; it has no idea which way is up. Release
  the sticks and it departs. This is most FPV freestyle hardware.
- **L1 — IMU + barometer.** Add an accelerometer reference and a baro and you get
  self-leveling and altitude hold (Betaflight ANGLE, or INAV). The failure mode
  that keeps it off L2: with no position source it still **drifts horizontally** —
  a gust walks it across the room.
- **L2 — a position source.** Either GPS outdoors (a Pixhawk-class FC — Holybro
  Pixhawk 6C / Cube Orange — with an M10 receiver, ArduPilot Loiter) **or**, for
  GPS-denied position hold, an **optical-flow sensor (PMW3901) plus a 1-D
  rangefinder** (TFmini / VL53L1x). It holds a point and can "sense and warn" off
  the rangefinder. Failure mode: flow dies over low-texture or low-light floors,
  and the 1-D rangefinder *lies* over edges and stairwells — it cannot avoid, only
  warn. That gap is the wall before L3.
- **L3 — onboard avoidance compute + a 2-D/3-D obstacle sensor.** Add a companion
  computer (Raspberry Pi 5 for light payloads, **Jetson Orin Nano** when you need
  the TOPS) and a real obstacle sensor — a 2-D LiDAR (RPLiDAR A2/A3) or a stereo
  depth camera (Intel RealSense D435i) — feeding ArduPilot's BendyRuler/Dijkstra
  avoidance. Now it senses *and avoids* along a set route. Failure mode: it plans
  in 2-D or short-range depth, in good light, in a known area — push it into a
  dark unmapped 3-D volume and it stops or clips.
- **L4A — 3-D LiDAR + real-time LIO-SLAM.** This is the GPS-denied, build-the-map
  level. Minimum: a **Jetson Orin NX 16GB** (or AGX Orin) running **FAST-LIO2 /
  Wildcat-class LIO-SLAM** on a **3-D LiDAR — Livox Mid-360, Ouster OS0, or Hesai
  XT32**. Fielded examples: Skydio X10, Exyn ExynAero, Emesent Hovermap ST-X.
  Failure mode: **degenerate geometry** — a long featureless tunnel or a smooth
  silo gives the LIO nothing to lock to and the estimate slides. See
  [Perception](perception.md) for why, and how absolute fixes bound it.
- **L4B — semantics, not just geometry.** Reasoning about *what* an obstacle is.
  Minimum: a higher-TOPS **Jetson AGX Orin** plus sensing that survives degraded
  visuals — **multi-return LiDAR** (penetrates light dust) or **mmWave radar**,
  fused with thermal/RGB, running onboard classification. The point is to fly
  *through* dust it recognizes as dust while still stopping for the branch it
  recognizes as solid. Failure mode: a misclassification here is worse than no
  classification — flying into a "dust cloud" that was a wall. The honest fallback
  is to degrade to pure geometric avoidance when confidence drops (see below).
- **L4C — objective-level reasoning in heavy DVE.** Minimum (and this is the
  bleeding edge): an **AGX Orin 64GB** with **LWIR thermal (FLIR Boson+)** + RGB +
  LiDAR and a VLM-class onboard model that reasons about the *mission* — "search
  this smoke-filled floor and find people." Largely research-grade today (Exyn's
  Level 4C is aspirational); this is the target for **Prismo APB**.
- **L5 — no hardware suffices.** There is no 2026 sensor suite or compute that
  delivers all-conditions, any-environment full autonomy. L5 is the asymptote, not
  a product. Anyone selling it is selling L4 with the conditions filed off.

**Where the line really is.** Fielded, buy-it-today systems top out around
**4A** (Exyn, Emesent, Skydio X10). Semantic **4B** is emerging in the best
platforms. **4C** is research. **5** does not exist. A useful gut check: if a
vendor claims 4B+ but ships a single RGB camera and no thermal/LiDAR/radar, the
hardware can't back the claim.

## The fallback row is the part that keeps it flying

The fallback column ties directly to the **confidence-driven authority** model in
[Onboard AI & Control](onboard-ai-control.md): autonomy should not silently
degrade when its inputs do — it should *know* they degraded and *change behavior*.
A level claim is only as good as its defined minimal-risk condition:

- **Link loss** — L2/L3 fall back to position-hold or RTL; L4 continues the tasked
  mission (the whole point of out-of-loop autonomy is surviving link denial) but
  drops to a safe-hold if state estimation itself fails.
- **State-estimation loss** (SLAM/flow drops out) — stop and hold, don't keep
  flying on a dead estimate. This is the L4 equivalent of L1's "level and wait."
- **Confidence drop** at L4B/4C — degrade *down* a level: semantic → geometric →
  hold. Never up. An uncertain classifier flying *through* an obstacle is the
  worst outcome on the chart.
- **Low battery** — minimal-risk landing always wins over mission completion,
  every level.

The benchmarks that tell you whether your state estimate is honest enough to
trust at L4 live in [Datasets & Benchmarks](datasets.md) — replay ground truth
through your estimator before you let it fly the fallback logic.
