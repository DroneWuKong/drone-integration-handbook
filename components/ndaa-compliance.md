# Federal UAS Procurement Screening Guide

> **Verified:** July 31, 2026  
> **Primary scope:** U.S. executive-agency procurement and operation screening under the American Security Drone Act implementation in FAR Subpart 40.2.  
> **Use:** Preliminary acquisition screening—not a legal opinion, product certification, Blue List determination, contracting-officer decision, export classification, or solicitation-specific representation.

There is no single universal “NDAA compliant” test that can be answered from a manufacturer name, country label, assembly location, marketing statement, or component list alone.

A defensible procurement conclusion depends on:

- the buyer and agency;
- the source and type of funds;
- the transaction being acquired;
- the current official covered-entity information;
- the manufacturer and assembler of the complete UAS;
- the exact configuration proposed;
- the solicitation and incorporated clauses;
- agency, program, mission, cybersecurity, and supply-chain requirements;
- any exemption, exception, or waiver;
- the date of the determination.

Use this guide to organize that review and preserve an evidence record. The final determination belongs to the responsible contracting officer, program office, counsel, grant administrator, or other authorized decision-maker.

---

## What FAR Subpart 40.2 currently does

FAR Subpart 40.2 implements the American Security Drone Act of 2023 for federal acquisitions.

As of the verification date:

- It applies to executive-agency acquisitions, including acquisitions at or below the micro-purchase threshold and acquisitions of commercial products or commercial services.
- It defines an **American Security Drone Act-covered foreign entity** by reference to the list developed by the Federal Acquisition Security Council and published in SAM.gov.
- It defines a **FASC-prohibited unmanned aircraft system** as a UAS manufactured or assembled by an ASDA-covered foreign entity.
- Unless an exemption, exception, or waiver applies, executive agencies are prohibited from procuring a FASC-prohibited UAS.
- On and after December 22, 2025, the prohibition also reaches procurement of services for operating a FASC-prohibited UAS and use of federal funds for procurement or operation of such a UAS.
- FAR 40.202-8 requires the clause at FAR 52.240-1 in solicitations and contracts.
- The FAR provisions include specified exemptions, exceptions, case-specific waivers, documentation requirements, and proposal-assessment procedures.

The current FAR authority is time-limited and states that the applicable ASDA authorities expire on December 22, 2028 unless changed by law.

## The current official covered-entity starting point

The FAR definition points to the FASC list published in SAM.gov. The active SAM announcement states that the ASDA covered-foreign-entity list currently includes the entities on the U.S. Government's **Consolidated Screening List** and does not presently add other entities.

That means the first official screen is:

1. Open the current [SAM.gov ASDA covered-entity announcement](https://sam.gov/announcements/american-security-drone-act-covered-foreign-entity-list).
2. Search the current [Consolidated Screening List](https://www.trade.gov/consolidated-screening-list).
3. Search the legal entity, parent, subsidiaries, former names, trade names, and relevant manufacturer/assembler identities.
4. Save the search date, search terms, result, and evidence copy.

### What a list result means

| Result | Appropriate statement |
|---|---|
| Entity found | Potential ASDA/FAR prohibition; escalate immediately and identify exact listing and relationship to the proposed UAS |
| Entity not found | “Not located in the official screen on [date] using [terms]” |
| Identity uncertain | “Unable to complete the official screen because manufacturer/assembler identity is unresolved” |

**Not found is not the same as universally compliant.** The solicitation, configuration, other statutes, agency rules, cybersecurity requirements, export controls, sanctions, and ownership/control issues may still matter.

## Screening workflow

### Step 1 — Identify the buyer and authority

Record:

- executive agency, DoD component, state/local/tribal entity, educational institution, commercial buyer, or other customer;
- contracting office or grant administrator;
- whether the transaction is a federal contract, subcontract, cooperative agreement, grant, purchase-card transaction, direct commercial sale, or other instrument;
- applicable acquisition regulation and supplements.

Do not assume that FAR contract clauses automatically govern every grant or state/local purchase. Do not assume federal funding is irrelevant either. Review the actual award terms, program guidance, statutes, and flow-down obligations.

### Step 2 — Identify what is being acquired or funded

Describe the transaction precisely:

- complete UAS;
- aircraft only;
- ground-control equipment;
- payload or communications system;
- replacement components;
- software or cloud service;
- operation-as-a-service;
- maintenance, training, support, or data service;
- modification of an existing fleet.

FAR 40.202 uses the statutory UAS definition, which includes the unmanned aircraft and associated elements—including communication links and components required for safe and efficient operation in the National Airspace System. The exact boundary should be resolved for the proposed acquisition rather than reduced to a generic parts checklist.

### Step 3 — Resolve manufacturer and assembler identity

For the complete system and relevant associated elements, collect:

- legal manufacturer;
- final assembler;
- parent company and controlling entities;
- product model and revision;
- manufacturing and assembly locations;
- supplier declarations and bills of material;
- firmware, cloud, data-routing, and update providers;
- reseller versus manufacturer roles.

Country of origin can be relevant evidence, but country, founder birthplace, headquarters, reseller location, and assembly location are not interchangeable legal tests.

### Step 4 — Perform and preserve the official covered-entity screen

Use the current SAM/FASC information and Consolidated Screening List. Preserve:

```text
Screen date and time:
Search system/version:
Legal name searched:
Aliases/trade names searched:
Parent/subsidiaries searched:
Result:
Listing identifier, if found:
Reviewer:
Evidence location:
```

Re-run the screen before award and whenever the entity, ownership, product configuration, or official lists change.

### Step 5 — Read the solicitation and incorporated clauses

At minimum, check for:

- FAR 52.240-1;
- FAR Part 40 requirements;
- agency acquisition-regulation supplements;
- DoD or service-specific clauses and memoranda;
- cybersecurity, data-hosting, software-update, and supply-chain requirements;
- domestic-preference and country-of-origin clauses;
- grant or award conditions;
- representations and certifications required from the offeror;
- subcontractor and flow-down requirements.

A solicitation may impose a narrower or broader practical requirement than a general marketing statement such as “NDAA compliant.” Answer the solicitation actually issued.

### Step 6 — Check exemptions, exceptions, and waivers

FAR 40.202 contains specific exemptions and exceptions for defined agencies and mission circumstances, plus a case-by-case waiver process. These are not self-executing permissions for a vendor or operator.

For any claimed exemption, exception, or waiver, record:

- exact FAR provision or other authority;
- authorized official;
- mission and scope;
- affected system/configuration;
- duration;
- conditions;
- written approval;
- contract-file documentation.

The FAR requires the contracting officer to document applicable exemptions, exceptions, or waivers and to coordinate their treatment in the solicitation and contract.

### Step 7 — Treat the Blue List as a separate DoD evidence source

DCMA's Blue List is a continuously vetted Department of Defense acquisition resource for unmanned systems and components. DCMA assumed responsibility for the program from DIU and operates a vendor/assessor process.

For a DoD acquisition:

- check the current [DCMA Blue List](https://www.dcma.mil/Blue-List/) or official Blue List portal;
- confirm the exact model, configuration, component, and status date;
- determine whether the solicitation requires or prefers Blue List status;
- verify whether modifications, payloads, radios, software, or data services alter the reviewed configuration;
- preserve the official record.

Do not use these shortcuts:

- **Blue List entry → eligible for every federal procurement:** not established.
- **No Blue List entry → prohibited for every procurement:** not established.
- **Manufacturer has one listed product → every product/configuration is listed:** not established.
- **Blue List status → export classification, FOCI, or every cybersecurity requirement resolved:** not established.

### Step 8 — Evaluate the exact configuration and data path

Record the fielded configuration, not just a base airframe name:

- aircraft revision and serial range;
- flight controller and firmware;
- radio and communications links;
- ground-control hardware/software;
- camera, gimbal, and payloads;
- navigation and obstacle-avoidance systems;
- data storage, cloud, update, and telemetry endpoints;
- batteries and propulsion where relevant to the solicitation;
- third-party modifications;
- remote-support access;
- software bill of materials and update authority.

A procurement conclusion should identify the configuration it covers and state that unreviewed substitutions or modifications may require a new determination.

### Step 9 — Obtain the authorized determination

The screening record should end with an authorized disposition, such as:

```text
Acquisition/solicitation:
Agency/program:
System and exact configuration:
Official list screen date:
Blue List status/date, if relevant:
Applicable clauses:
Exemption/exception/waiver:
Open questions:
Decision authority:
Decision:
Conditions:
Expiration/reverification date:
```

The handbook does not make this final determination.

## Evidence labels for public product profiles

Use one of the following labels instead of an unqualified compliance badge:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Official-list verified** | Exact product/entity status located in a named official source on a stated date |
| **Manufacturer-reported** | Vendor makes the claim; independent official confirmation not established |
| **Configuration documented** | Evidence collected for a named configuration; final eligibility still depends on the acquisition |
| **Program-specific approval reported** | Approval is tied to a named program or authority and should not be generalized |
| **Not located in official screen** | Search did not locate the entity/product on the named date; not a universal approval |
| **Unknown / unresolved** | Evidence is missing, contradictory, stale, or insufficient |
| **Counsel or contracting-officer determination required** | The handbook cannot responsibly resolve the issue |

Every label should display its source and verification date.

## Minimum procurement record

### Buyer and transaction

- [ ] Buyer, agency, program, and contracting office identified
- [ ] Contract, grant, subcontract, purchase-card, or other instrument identified
- [ ] Source of funds documented
- [ ] Product, service, operation, or modification scope documented

### Entity and product

- [ ] Legal manufacturer and assembler identified
- [ ] Parent, aliases, and relevant subsidiaries identified
- [ ] Exact model/revision/configuration identified
- [ ] Reseller distinguished from manufacturer

### Official sources

- [ ] SAM/FASC covered-entity information checked
- [ ] Consolidated Screening List searched and evidence preserved
- [ ] FAR 40.2 and FAR 52.240-1 applicability reviewed
- [ ] Agency supplements and solicitation clauses reviewed
- [ ] Current Blue List checked when relevant

### Exceptions and configuration

- [ ] Any exemption, exception, or waiver documented in writing
- [ ] Payloads, radios, GCS, firmware, cloud, and data paths reviewed
- [ ] Substitutions and modifications controlled
- [ ] Open questions escalated to the authorized decision-maker

### Decision

- [ ] Authorized reviewer identified
- [ ] Decision and conditions recorded
- [ ] Reverification date assigned
- [ ] Evidence retained with the acquisition file

## Common mistakes

**"The vendor says NDAA compliant."**  
Treat that as a manufacturer representation requiring defined scope and supporting evidence—not the final federal determination.

**"The company is American, so the system is eligible."**  
Headquarters alone does not resolve the legal manufacturer, assembler, ownership, configuration, associated elements, solicitation clauses, or official-list screen.

**"The company was not found on the FASC screen, so the procurement is approved."**  
A negative list search is one documented input, not a universal approval.

**"The system is on the Blue List, so every variant and payload is cleared."**  
Confirm the exact listed system/configuration and whether modifications remain within the reviewed scope.

**"The FAR rule only matters for large contracts."**  
FAR 40.202 states that it applies to all acquisitions, including those at or below the micro-purchase threshold and commercial acquisitions.

**"An exception obviously applies because the mission is important."**  
Use the exact authority and obtain the required written determination. Vendors and operators do not self-approve exemptions or waivers.

**"A country or founder's birthplace proves FOCI or compliance."**  
It does not. Ownership, control, influence, contracting requirements, and security determinations require actual evidence and the applicable review process.

**"NDAA, ASDA, Blue List, export classification, sanctions, and cybersecurity are the same test."**  
They are separate regimes and evidence streams. A conclusion under one should not be copied into another field.
