## Review objective

Permanently replace the public drone-to-drone intercept playbook with guidance for civilians, commercial operators, facilities, venues, educators, and public-safety organizations responding to an unfamiliar, unsafe, or suspicious drone.

The replacement is limited to:

- protecting people and the operating area;
- suspending the observer's own UAS operation;
- avoiding additional aircraft and collision hazards;
- reporting immediate danger or suspected crime to local law enforcement;
- reporting apparent FAA violations to the FAA/FSDO;
- preserving objective observations and evidence;
- handling fallen-aircraft and damaged-battery hazards conservatively;
- planning facility and event response;
- distinguishing FAA airspace questions from property and underlying-conduct questions.

## Material corrections from the withdrawn version

- Removed aircraft build, motor, battery, propeller, camera, video, speed, and tuning specifications for interception.
- Removed scramble procedures, pursuit, head-on/stern/beam geometry, lead calculation, terminal approach, target selection, ramming, and collision instructions.
- Removed target-specific guidance for reconnaissance drones, FPV aircraft, loitering munitions, and one-way attack aircraft.
- Removed multi-interceptor tactics, frequency planning for intercept teams, and mission-training guidance.
- Removed language treating another drone as a weapon target or expendable objective.
- Replaced the article with non-interference, public protection, objective evidence, official reporting, and facility planning.

## Deliberately excluded scope

- No private or civil actor is represented as having authority to defeat another UAS.
- No shooting, jamming, spoofing, protocol takeover, capture, netting, ramming, forced landing, directed energy, laser use, or pursuit is described.
- No weapon, payload, dangerous item, terminal procedure, or aircraft-damage method is described.
- No law-enforcement tactic or statutory counter-UAS authority is defined.
- No public article is presented as authorization for federal, state, local, tribal, military, intelligence, or security operations.
- No Remote ID observation is treated as permission to identify, confront, track, trespass against, or publicly expose an operator.
- No suspicious or unfamiliar aircraft is labeled hostile, criminal, military, or unlawful without an authorized finding.
